Why katakana

Japanese has three scripts and uses them for different jobs. Katakana is the one for foreign words — ラーメン, コーヒー, エンリコ・ブルーニ. So a Latin alphabet meant to read as Japanese should be drawn as katakana rather than as kanji: katakana is what a Japanese reader's eye already associates with foreign text.

That changes the drawing, not only the flavour. Katakana are one to four strokes, sparse and open; ロ is the only one that closes. Kanji are dense, often ten to twenty strokes packed into a grid. A Latin face built on kanji logic comes out as a wall of boxes. Built on katakana logic it comes out sparse, diagonal and open — which is what this is.

Four things carry it

Sweeps

Katakana have so few strokes that each one must carry the character, so it runs the width of the square and tapers to a needle. Every diagonal here is a 払い at that length — A K M N R V W X Y Z and their lowercase.

The フ turn

A hairline meets a hard 肩 corner and sweeps away. It is how フ ア ク ス ヌ マ ワ ラ all begin, and it builds 7, 2, Z, z and J.

Contrast

A 横画 is a hairline and a 縦画 is a slab — 46 units against 104 in the display cut, a ratio of 0.44. This is the loudest signal in the script.

Fit

The glyphs fill the square. Cap height is 790 of a 1000 em and the x-height is 600 — 0.76 of the cap, far higher than any Latin face — so lowercase sits square as kana do.

Katakana is the script Japanese uses for foreign words. A Latin alphabet meant to read as Japanese should therefore be drawn as katakana rather than as kanji, because katakana is what a reader's eye already associates with foreign text.

That is a structural claim, not a decorative one. Katakana are one to four strokes, sparse and open, and ロ is the only character that closes. A face built on kanji logic comes out dense; built on katakana logic it comes out airy and diagonal.

The text cut lowers the contrast so the hairlines survive, thickens the terminals, and quiets the pressure modulation. It is an optical size, not a lighter weight.

The display cut keeps the contrast high, at 0.44. Set large it is the more elegant of the two; set small its horizontals thin out, which is the trade the text cut exists to solve.

Bold

Mangafont Text Bold

Contrast falls as weight rises, because it does in Mincho too. Holding 0.44 into a bold would leave hairlines that snap at any real size, so the bolds sit at 0.51 and 0.64.

Mangafont Bold

Terminals grow sub-proportionally, on a 0.72 power law. An うろこ scaled linearly into a bold swallows the letter it is sitting on.
