Three directions for the comic

All three are real working fonts, built from the same katakana skeletons — so whichever you pick already has the full Latin alphabet and the katakana block. They differ in register: how the strokes are weighted and finished. Tell me a letter and I'll build it out into a proper family.

Caps carry a comic — balloons are traditionally all-caps — but each one is shown in lowercase too, for captions and narration.

ABrush Sumi ink. Huge contrast, dramatic tapers, big scale-flags. The calligraphic end.

Why this one for a comic: it holds up at balloon size, survives cheap printing, and the overshooting strokes are what make it read as kanji at a glance rather than on inspection.
