In text

A glyph here is not a shape but a sequence of brush strokes. Each stroke is a centreline, a pressure profile along it, and a pair of terminals; the outline is derived by offsetting. Nothing is drawn as a closed contour by hand.

Bowls are chamfered rectangles rather than circles, and the side bearings are wide and nearly even, so a line of text blocks up the way Japanese text does instead of flowing.

Where a Latin letter has an exact counterpart in the source script, it is drawn as that character and not as the letter — which is why the alphabet holds together at all.
